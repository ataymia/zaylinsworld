# ZTA World Visual Reference Bible

Status: authoritative visual companion to all town blueprints  
Project: Zaylins Kid World, also called ZTA  
Scope: concept-art translation, skyline, district composition, roads, filler, biomes, landmarks, signage, color, atmosphere, and master-map continuity

This document converts the approved town concept images and master world map into implementation language so their visual richness is not lost when the world is built.

The images are not disposable mood boards. They establish the intended density, geography, silhouette, atmosphere, district contrast, road complexity, civic completeness, and sense of place for every town.

---

## 1. Authority and naming rules

The visual reference and the written town blueprint are used together.

```txt
Written blueprint defines:
- canonical town and building names
- gameplay function
- dimensions and coordinates
- unlock logic
- district systems
- jobs, school, law, housing, and services

Visual reference defines:
- composition
- skyline
- density
- atmosphere
- district contrast
- road feeling
- filler massing
- visual storytelling
- landmark prominence
```

If generated poster text conflicts with an established canonical name, the established blueprint name wins unless Mia explicitly promotes the poster name.

Examples:

- Tech Academy visual role maps to canonical VoltByte Academy.
- Rich Hills Academy visual role maps to canonical Legacy Preparatory Academy.
- Hospitality Academy visual role maps to canonical BrightHouse Hospitality & Games Institute.
- Poster hotel or game-hall names may become secondary establishments, signs, wings, event spaces, or future named venues without replacing locked names automatically.

The concept art must not be simplified into one hero building surrounded by empty terrain.

---

## 2. Shared art direction

### Camera and readability

Town concept and implementation views should support:

- strong three-quarter aerial readability;
- clear district silhouettes;
- visible major roads and secondary routes;
- recognizable landmarks from a distance;
- dense but organized environmental storytelling;
- readable transitions between public, residential, industrial, civic, school, and service zones;
- roads that communicate where a player can drive without making the city feel like a diagram.

### Game-ready style

The intended world style is:

```txt
stylized 3D
colorful
high-detail at hero locations
slightly simplified geometry at distance
clean silhouettes
readable materials
family-friendly open-world realism
strong signage and landmark identity
lived-in rather than sterile
```

It should not become photorealistic, tiny toy dioramas, flat Roblox blocks, or empty procedural terrain.

### Density rule

Every concept image contains three layers:

1. **Hero layer:** major civic, school, entertainment, service, and residential landmarks.
2. **District layer:** roads, blocks, parking, parks, storefronts, homes, warehouses, docks, and public space.
3. **Life layer:** cars, boats, signs, delivery areas, lights, fences, loading equipment, benches, trees, utility objects, NPC gathering areas, and route markers.

All three layers are required.

### Signage rule

Signs should help players read geography rather than cover every surface.

Use:

- gateway signs;
- district wayfinding;
- landmark names;
- road and transit signs;
- service signs;
- safety and restriction signs;
- maps at major arrivals;
- visual color coding by town.

Avoid floating labels in normal gameplay unless part of map UI, navigation, or accessibility settings.

# 3. Starter Town Visual Specification

## Core image essence

Starter Town must feel like a complete, welcoming, slightly imperfect American-style everyday city where the player can actually build a life. It is not a tiny tutorial block. It is a 2,000 x 2,000 reference city with visible work, school, civic, retail, industrial, recreational, and residential geography.

The approved visual composition shows the historic city core in the middle, neighborhoods and recreation toward the lower city, school to the west, auto and industrial land to the north, civic hills to the northeast, and highway services to the east.

## Required city silhouette

From a distance, the city should read through:

- a central historic downtown clocktower or civic silhouette;
- WorkTower rising from Civic Heights;
- police/public-safety buildings on a hill;
- a school campus and stadium/field;
- Northworks warehouses and highway ramps;
- the Eastgate gas-station canopy and parkway signs;
- a large park and gym complex;
- extensive residential roofs and winding streets;
- the Dreamdrop Beltway surrounding the city;
- distant waterworks, reservoir, dam, or river infrastructure along the world edge where geographically compatible.

## District composition

### Dreamdrop District

Visual requirements:

- older brick storefront blocks;
- a recognizable crossroads;
- town clock, small civic tower, fountain, or round public landmark;
- mixed-height buildings rather than one flat strip;
- crosswalks, curb parking, buses, delivery vans, alleys, and traffic lights;
- Frostbox and Chicken Spot embedded in a broader commercial fabric;
- service lanes and back-of-building loading areas;
- pedestrians and ordinary city clutter.

Dreamdrop should feel historic and dense, but not like a giant metropolitan downtown.

### Market Mile

Visual requirements:

- longer commercial corridor;
- Kicks & Fits and other retail façades;
- larger parking areas mixed with walkable shopfronts;
- signs, awnings, small plazas, bus stops, and loading bays;
- a transition from downtown bricks to newer commercial buildings;
- back service roads that allow delivery and crime gameplay.

### Northworks / Auto Row

Visual requirements:

- Auto Haus dealership lot;
- City Garage and repair bays;
- warehouse blocks;
- tow yard;
- service trucks and parked vehicles;
- highway interchange or elevated ramp geometry;
- wider roads narrowing into industrial lanes;
- fencing, loading docks, utility poles, storage, and signs;
- a visual sense of acceleration and driving risk.

### Scholar's Quarter

Visual requirements:

- Zaylins Prep as a real campus;
- sports field, track, court, bus loop, or recreation field;
- school-zone roads and raised crossings;
- nearby homes and small community buildings;
- water tower or utility landmark;
- school buses and protected pedestrian routes;
- library/community-learning visual support.

### Civic Heights

Visual requirements:

- WorkTower as a major skyline anchor;
- police station/public-safety campus;
- civic circle, plaza, or roundabout;
- hill roads, switchbacks, retaining walls, and guardrails;
- trees and landscaping that feel maintained but public;
- views back toward downtown;
- road geometry that teaches downhill braking and difficult turns.

### Eastgate Corridor

Visual requirements:

- 6twelve or the canonical fuel-service landmark;
- highway and parkway signage;
- merge lanes;
- service businesses and filler commercial parcels;
- a clear gateway toward Rich Hills;
- faster traffic than the central city;
- convenience parking and visible fuel canopy.

### Parkside Commons

Visual requirements:

- Iron City Gym;
- large public park;
- pond or water feature where feasible;
- playground, courts, paths, picnic structures, and trees;
- curved roads and a roundabout;
- community event space;
- parking that does not consume the entire district.

### Willowbend Residential

Visual requirements:

- the starter home visibly located inside a real neighborhood;
- detached homes, duplexes, apartments, and townhouses;
- cul-de-sacs, curved local streets, alleys, driveways, and parked cars;
- fences, mailboxes, yards, trees, and utility lines;
- enough travel distance that leaving home feels like starting a commute;
- house-buying and property markers that fit naturally into the neighborhood.

### Westside Blocks

Visual requirements:

- working-class mixed housing;
- Block Supply transition area;
- small commercial corners;
- apartments and older homes;
- garages, back lots, and service roads;
- visual bridge between Scholar's Quarter, Market Mile, and the outer city.

## Road image requirements

The concept art establishes that Starter Town must contain:

- an outer Beltway;
- major arterials;
- highway ramps;
- a real downtown crossroads;
- roundabouts;
- curved residential streets;
- school-zone streets;
- industrial service roads;
- hilly civic roads;
- multiple route choices across the city.

The player should be able to see that home, school, work, police, auto services, shopping, and recreation occupy different parts of the city.

## Color and mood

- bright blue sky;
- green hills and trees;
- warm brick downtown;
- cool civic blue and gray;
- industrial neutral metal;
- welcoming residential creams, blues, browns, and muted colors;
- clear daylight as the default visual showcase.

## Prohibited simplification

Starter Town cannot ship as:

- four blocks and a circle;
- all functional buildings facing one intersection;
- a flat lawn between destinations;
- one residential street;
- a straight two-second drive from home to every job.

# 4. Fishing Harbor Visual Specification

## Core image essence

Fishing Harbor is a charming but hardworking coastal municipality. Its beauty comes from active docks, a layered shoreline, houses climbing rocky roads, markets, patrol, school, fuel, fishing infrastructure, and water routes.

It should feel like morning air, salt, ropes, wet boards, engines, fish markets, gulls, and a town whose daily life depends on the tide.

## Required silhouette

- tall striped lighthouse on a rocky point;
- Harbor Academy on higher ground;
- dense waterfront market buildings;
- Harbor Patrol beside a patrol dock;
- boat masts and fishing vessels;
- TideFuel positioned between road and water service;
- village apartments and waterfront homes on hills;
- fisher cottages along a quieter shore;
- outer islands or mountain silhouettes across the water.

## Required visual districts and elements

### Market Wharf

- prominent seafood market;
- bait-and-tackle shop;
- Dockside Diner over or beside the water;
- public fishing pier;
- seafood crates, ice, scales, stalls, and delivery lanes;
- people moving between boats and market;
- signs and awnings that feel weathered rather than glossy.

### Working Docks

- fishing boats;
- small rental boats;
- dock slips;
- fuel dock;
- stacked traps;
- net-drying racks;
- ropes, barrels, crates, and service sheds;
- work areas separated from tourist paths;
- cold-storage and boatworks cues.

### Marina

- rental office;
- player boat slips;
- marked boat routes;
- no-wake buoys;
- vehicle parking near ramps;
- water lanes visible through buoys or navigation lights;
- safe docking geometry.

### Residential Coast

- waterfront homes;
- village apartments;
- fisher cottages;
- winding cliff roads;
- stairs and footpaths to water;
- retaining walls and rocky shore;
- a visual difference between ordinary fishing homes and premium waterfront property.

### Harbor Academy and Civic Zone

- academy campus visible above the harbor;
- Harbor Patrol office with both road and dock access;
- public map and permit information;
- clinic and civic services integrated into the town;
- town square or small civic crossroads.

### Lighthouse Point

- lighthouse and keeper building;
- rocky trail;
- protected water and nesting cues;
- rare-catch zone;
- hidden-cove or shipwreck exploration language;
- weather and rescue equipment.

## Fishing progression storytelling

The visual environment should support:

- beginner fishing spots near town;
- marked boat routes;
- deeper rare-catch waters;
- hidden coves;
- shipwrecks;
- protected zones;
- the Gillyfish discovery route;
- catch boards showing that fish can be ordinary, rare, strange, or powerful.

A harbor map or public navigation board should communicate these layers.

## Color and mood

# 5. Rich Hills Visual Specification

Rich Hills is a luxury city built on dramatic elevation. Its visual power comes from switchback estate roads, terraces, golf greens, marina water, gated security, civic prestige, luxury retail, and the visible service economy beneath it.

It must look expensive without pretending workers, warehouses, fuel, police, and maintenance disappear.

## Required silhouette

- multiple ridgelines of estates;
- a summit or upper ridge neighborhood;
- a country club and golf course at the heart of the hills;
- scenic cliff estate or overlook;
- academy and police buildings around a formal civic plaza;
- Sovereign-style gated entrance;
- CrestFuel canopy;
- marina and yachts at the bottom edge;
- service village clearly visible below luxury districts.

## Required visual subareas

### Summit Ridge and Estate Roads

- large homes at different elevations;
- switchback roads;
- gates and private drives;
- retaining walls;
- cypress, palm, hedge, and ornamental landscaping;
- pools, balconies, and terraces;
- scenic overlooks and cliff houses;
- readable public versus private boundaries.

### Country Club

- broad green fairways;
- sand traps and ponds;
- prominent clubhouse;
- valet circle;
- golf-cart paths;
- event lawn;
- maintenance access hidden from the public front but physically present.

### Civic Crest

- Legacy Preparatory Academy in a prestigious campus role;
- Rich Hills Police beside or near it;
- formal plaza, crest, fountain, landscaping, and roundabout;
- clinic and public services nearby;
- architecture that feels civic and expensive, not royal fantasy.

### Luxury Row

- Crownline Motors;
- real-estate office;
- galleries, fashion, jewelry, cafés;
- valet and drop-off lanes;
- palm-lined sidewalks;

### Harbor View and Marina

- marina fuel and repair;
- waterfront roads and walking path;
- visible connection to Crownwater Basin.

### Service Village

# 6. TechTown Visual Specification

TechTown is a dense, optimistic smart city where advanced systems are visible in public life. The city should look inventive, expensive, efficient, and busy, but still human and playable.

- VoltByte Academy with strong shield or technology identity;
- GridLine Transit Hub with large curved roof and elevated rail;
- central technological monument or crystal-data spire;
- startup and lab clusters;
- ByteMart flagship;
- smart homes and premium vertical housing;
- VoltFuel and separate charge zone;
- drones in the sky;
- elevated transit and skyways;
- distant Cloudview skyline.

## Required visual subareas

### Innovation District

- VoltByte Academy;
- Innovate Labs;
- startup hub;
- co-working and code spaces;
- plazas and screens;
- drone activity;
- clean sidewalks and crossings;
- central public technology monument.

### Transit Core

- GridLine Transit Hub;
- elevated train or maglev;
- transit tracks crossing the city;
- stations and platforms;
- pedestrian circulation beneath or beside infrastructure;
- delivery and maintenance access.

### Retail and Smart Residential

- ByteMart electronics flagship;
- smart homes with solar roofs and rooftop greenery;
- rooftop condos;
- delivery lockers;
- smart parking;
- smaller service shops;
- visible residential life rather than only offices.

### Cloudview District

The concept art introduces a premium vertical skyline role called **Cloudview District**.

Cloudview should be treated as a subdistrict within the premium residential/corporate skyline area, containing:

- rooftop residences;
- high-rise smart apartments;
- elevated gardens;
- skyway access;
- premium offices;
- skyline observation points;
- higher-security access zones.

### Security Layer

- corporate security separated from public police;

### VoltFuel and Charge Zone

- delivery depot nearby;

### GridLink Visual Presence

The city should visually foreshadow teleportation through:

- sync beacons;
- clean geometric rings;
- mobility-lab signage;
- Gadget Forge display;

- GridLink service kiosk at the transit hub after ownership.

# 11. Aqualume Visual Specification

## Core image essence

Aqualume is a luminous underwater capital that feels ancient, advanced, civic, ecological, and alive. It is the final earned city and must look more astonishing than every surface town without abandoning functionality.

The approved image establishes a radial city of glass tunnels, pearl domes, civic spires, coral markets, homes, generators, transit, mini-sub docks, sea-scooter routes, gardens, and Current Guard protection.

- Tideglass Academy;
- Current Guard fortress/civic building;
- CurrentShift generator towers;
- Coral Market;
- Reefside Homes;
- mini-sub docks;

Central civic crown

# 12. Master World Map Visual Specification

## Purpose

The master map communicates the relationship between towns, biomes, routes, water, and progression. It is a visual planning poster, not a replacement for authoritative world coordinates.

The exact coordinates remain defined in:

- `LARGE_WORLD_SCALE_BLUEPRINT.md`
- `src/config/worldMapPlan.js`

The poster establishes the emotional geography and relative world story.

## Relative arrangement

```txt
Far north:
Starline City

North-central:
Casino Strip
Fishing Harbor

East-northeast:
TechTown

Far east:
Dungeon Outskirts

Far east / farther south or north by route according to authoritative coordinates:
Obby Canyon

Center / southern hub:
Starter Town

East / southeast of Starter:
Rich Hills

Under Crownwater Basin between the coastal powers:
Aqualume
```

Implementation uses the locked coordinate plan even if a poster compresses distances for readability.

## Required world features

### Starter Town Hub

- visually largest normal city footprint;
- multiple roads radiating outward;
- beltway and skyline;
- central geographic and narrative role;
- nearby mixed grassland, forest, river, or utility terrain.

### Crownwater Basin

- large shared body of water;
- Fishing Harbor on the working coast;
- Rich Hills marina on the luxury coast;
- Casino bridge/coast connection;
- underwater route to Aqualume;
- boats, yachts, reefs, and deep-water markings;
- not a decorative blue strip.

### Mountain and Forest Regions

- mountain corridors around selected routes;
- tunnels;
- forests and rivers;
- waterfalls and reservoirs where compatible;
- enough terrain between towns to justify multi-minute travel.

### Desert and Canyon Regions

- Obby Canyon;
- arid routes toward Dungeon Outskirts;
- mesas, rock towers, dirt roads, and sparse service areas;
- environmental transition rather than sudden biome tiles.

### Snow or High-Elevation Visuals

The concept poster uses snow/tundra near the far-north entertainment region for map contrast. This is a visual-biome option, not a locked requirement that Starline itself must be snowy.

Possible implementation:

- distant northern mountains;
- high-elevation route;
- seasonal snow outside Starline;
- cool mountain corridor framing the warm city.

Starline City itself retains its warm cinematic visual identity.

## Route hierarchy

The map must visually distinguish:

- highways;
- parkways;
- bridges;
- tunnels;
- dirt roads;
- sea routes;
- underwater routes;
- town gateways;
- first-visit physical routes;
- later GridLink nodes.

GridLink destination markers appear only after purchase and synchronization. They do not replace roads on the map.

## Map UI evolution

### Before discovery

- town silhouette hidden or vague;
- route may be partially known;
- no GridLink node;
- no detailed district map.

### After physical discovery

- town icon and name revealed;
- physical route saved;
- district map becomes available;
- arrival node may be synchronized.

### After GridLink ownership

- synchronized nodes gain GridLink icon;
- cooldown status shown;
- blocked destinations explain requirements;
- Aqualume remains hidden/locked until its earned progression is complete.

# 13. Cross-town visual continuity

Every town must feel different, but the world must feel authored by one game.

Shared continuity comes from:

- consistent road-marking language;
- readable police and emergency icons;
- compatible map symbols;
- recurring fuel/charge service logic with unique town architecture;
- consistent property markers;
- consistent school and job navigation rules;
- recurring phone and map UI;
- coherent vehicle scale;
- shared environmental quality and level of filler detail;
- recurring Zaylins character proportions and animation language.

Town-specific identity comes from:

- palette;
- terrain;
- skyline;
- road geometry;
- civic architecture;
- housing;
- transit type;
- signage;
- vegetation;
- ambient objects;
- jobs and public behavior.

# 14. Visual implementation gate

A town cannot pass visual completion if it contains only its named functional buildings.

Required visual acceptance questions:

```txt
Can the town be identified from its skyline alone?
Can a player recognize districts without opening the map?
Are home, school, work, police, fuel, shopping, and recreation geographically separated?
Do roads create meaningful route choice?
Does the town include visible workers and support infrastructure?
Does residential life feel real?
Are industrial and service areas physically present?
Are major concept-image landmarks represented?
Is the color palette controlled and recognizable?
Does filler reinforce town identity rather than repeat generic blocks?
Can the player understand how the town connects to the larger world?
```
